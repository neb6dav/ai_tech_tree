Research questions and open direction

Open direction

Can a system retain broad language-model coverage while making its consequential inferences machine-checkable?

Closure criteria

On a predeclared suite spanning mathematics, repository-scale software verification, and structured domain rules, report proof coverage, proof-check success, residual unverified claims, abstentions, and capability loss relative to an otherwise matched unconstrained model.

Claim state: Editorial hypothesis · High confidence

Partial results

  • DeepSeek-Prover-V2 demonstrates strong formal mathematics performance in Lean 4.
  • LeanDojo and ReProver make retrieval-augmented theorem proving reproducible at laboratory scale.
  • VeriSoftBench shows that mathematics-specialized provers transfer poorly to dependency-rich software repositories.

Counterexamples and limits

  • DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B reports 88.9 percent on MiniF2F and solves 49 of 658 PutnamBench problems.
  • LeanDojo and ReProver provide open data, tooling, retrieval, and a 98,734-theorem benchmark.
  • FormalMATH, Leanabell-Prover-V2, and Lean Copilot provide additional narrow formal-reasoning systems.

Crowdedness: High in formal mathematics and medium in cross-domain verification.

Tractability: High for benchmark and tooling work; low for broad end-to-end verification.

Suggested starting points

  • Individual: Build a proof-coverage auditor for a 7B prover on LeanDojo and VeriSoftBench that separates verified conclusions, unsupported premises, failed proofs, and timeouts.
  • Small lab: Train and evaluate one prover across mathematics and repository-scale software, explicitly measuring transfer loss and dependency-retrieval failures.
  • Well-funded lab: Develop a proof-carrying general assistant whose high-consequence outputs compile into multiple formal backends, with calibrated abstention when formalization is incomplete.
