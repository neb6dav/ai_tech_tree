Research questions and open direction

Questions

  • Can an AI system sustain repeated capability improvement across genuinely held-out domains without optimizing a fixed benchmark, depending on a hidden stronger teacher, or accumulating unacceptable safety regressions?
  • What would count as sustained general improvement rather than benchmark-specific optimization or evaluator exploitation?

Open direction

Can an AI system sustain repeated capability improvement across genuinely held-out domains without optimizing a fixed benchmark, depending on a hidden stronger teacher, or accumulating unacceptable safety regressions?

Closure criteria

Pre-register several improvement cycles and sealed domain families; show statistically reliable gains on newly unsealed tasks after every cycle; compare against static-agent, ordinary fine-tuning, search-only, and human-engineered baselines; publish compute, interventions, failures, contamination checks, and a safety-regression budget; and obtain an independent rerun.

Claim state: Editorial hypothesis · High confidence

Partial results

  • The Darwin Gödel Machine evolves coding-agent implementations and improved bounded software-engineering benchmarks, while SEAL learns its own update directives and training data for bounded adaptation tasks. AlphaEvolve and related verifier-guided systems offer further evidence that self-generated search can improve artifacts. None yet demonstrates safe, durable, domain-general improvement under repeatedly refreshed evaluation.

Counterexamples and limits

  • Darwin Gödel Machine is a concrete self-editing, archive-based coding agent; its gains are on bounded coding benchmarks and one reported run cost about USD 22,000 in API use over roughly two weeks.
  • SEAL generates its own update directives and fine-tuning data, but evaluates bounded knowledge-incorporation and few-shot tasks rather than indefinite cross-domain improvement.
  • Search over self-edit strategies and verifier-guided evolutionary systems show useful improvement, while also exposing homogenization, evaluator gaming, and dependence on externally supplied objectives.

Novelty review

Status: Targeted search. Checked: 2026-08-04. Targeted arXiv title-and-abstract searches for open-ended self-improvement, self-editing agents, self-adapting language models, automated coding-agent evolution, verifier-guided evolution, and 2025-2026 follow-on work; primary-paper reference chaining around DGM and SEAL. High confidence that the narrowed gap remains open. The evidence is strong for bounded self-improvement, but the search found no system satisfying repeated held-out cross-domain improvement, independence from a hidden stronger teacher, and bounded safety regressions together. This is a targeted review, not an exhaustive priority search or a priority claim.

Estimated effort: Individual: One to four modern GPUs for local 7B-14B models, or a tightly capped API budget; five to ten update cycles. · SmallLab: Eight to thirty-two GPUs, sandboxed execution, sealed multi-domain evaluations, and an independent evaluator. · WellFundedLab: Several repeated DGM-scale runs across model families; the DGM paper reports about two weeks and USD 22,000 API cost for one run, before external evaluation and red-team costs.

Crowdedness: High for self-improving agents and automated coding; lower for preregistered, repeatedly refreshed, cross-domain and safety-bounded trials.

Tractability: Medium for bounded coding experiments; low for a convincing general and safe long-horizon closure result.

Suggested starting points

  • Individual: Reproduce one DGM- or SEAL-style update loop on two disjoint small domains, freeze both evaluators before training, and publish every candidate, rollback, cost, and regression.
  • Small lab: Run a 10-cycle, multi-domain archive experiment with sealed future tasks and compare a static agent, self-editing agent, and diversity-preserving open-ended archive under the same inference and training budget.
  • Well-funded lab: Conduct a multi-model, long-horizon improvement trial with immutable external evaluations, independent red teaming, automatic rollback, full lineage, and a third-party audit of contamination and safety regressions.
