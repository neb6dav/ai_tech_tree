Research questions and open direction

Questions

  • Can an end-to-end learning workload show a reproducible quantum advantage over the strongest classical alternative after state preparation, data acquisition, error mitigation, wall-clock time, and energy are all counted?

Open direction

Can an end-to-end learning workload show a reproducible quantum advantage over the strongest classical alternative after state preparation, data acquisition, error mitigation, wall-clock time, and energy are all counted?

Closure criteria

Pre-register the access model, task distribution, hardware, classical comparison budget, and success threshold; include state-preparation, shots, calibration, error mitigation, queue and wall-clock costs; red-team the classical baseline; demonstrate scaling beyond feasible classical replication; and reproduce on independent hardware.

Claim state: Editorial hypothesis · High confidence

Partial results

  • Provable and experimental learning advantages already exist for carefully specified quantum-data access models, including learning from experiments. Broad empirical benchmarking still finds that ordinary classical models usually outperform tested quantum models on classical-data tasks. The defensible open question is practical end-to-end advantage under full resource accounting, not whether any quantum learning advantage has ever been proved.

Counterexamples and limits

  • Quantum Advantage in Learning from Experiments proves exponential sample advantages under specified quantum-data access and demonstrates experiments up to 40 superconducting qubits and roughly 1,300 gates; those assumptions do not automatically establish a practical advantage for ordinary classical data.
  • The qml-benchmarks study evaluates 12 quantum models across 160 datasets grouped into 10 benchmarks and generally finds tuned classical models stronger; simulations were limited to modest qubit counts.
  • Recent analyses report plausible noisy-qubit separations at tens of qubits, but they are not yet an independently replicated end-to-end hardware advantage with full acquisition and runtime accounting.

Novelty review

Status: Targeted search. Checked: 2026-08-04. Targeted arXiv searches for provable quantum learning advantage, learning from experiments, practical QML advantage, QML benchmarking, noisy-qubit separations, data-loading and state-preparation costs, plus reference chaining from the two representative papers. High confidence that the practical, fully accounted and independently replicated gap remains open. Existing theory proves advantages under specified access models, so this card makes no blanket no-advantage claim. The review makes no general quantum-supremacy or priority claim.

Estimated effort: Individual: CPU/GPU simulation up to roughly 18-24 qubits, depending on circuit and memory, plus classical tuning compute. · SmallLab: Cloud-QPU allocation, HPC simulation, and a separate classical-baseline team. · WellFundedLab: Reliable access to two 30-50-qubit hardware platforms, low-level experimental support, substantial HPC, and independent replication.

Crowdedness: Medium-high; QML is crowded, while transparent end-to-end cost accounting and adversarial classical baselines remain less common.

Tractability: High for rigorous small-scale benchmarking and valuable negative results; very low for a scalable practical-advantage closure result.

Suggested starting points

  • Individual: Reproduce selected qml-benchmarks with stronger classical hyperparameter search, identical data splits, and full training and inference timing; publish null results and sensitivity analyses.
  • Small lab: Implement one learning-from-experiments task on cloud quantum hardware, pre-register the best classical red team, and account for shots, calibration, state preparation, mitigation, transfer, queue, and wall-clock costs.
  • Well-funded lab: Run the same 30-50-qubit coherent-data learning experiment on two independent hardware stacks, with a separate classical-HPC team and audited time, energy, acquisition, and uncertainty accounting.
