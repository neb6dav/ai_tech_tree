Summary

Research direction: predict and test the data regimes, shifts, modalities and tuning budgets under which deep tabular models outperform strong boosted-tree ensembles. Deep models already win in some large, multimodal or representation-rich settings; a reliable regime-selection rule remains unresolved.

Research questions and open direction

Questions

  • Under equal tuning and total-cost budgets, which data regimes and distribution shifts reliably favor neural or tabular-foundation models over boosted trees, and which still favor trees?

Open direction

Under equal tuning and total-cost budgets, which data regimes and distribution shifts reliably favor neural or tabular-foundation models over boosted trees, and which still favor trees?

Closure criteria

Produce a leak-checked, preregistered regime map covering IID, temporal, grouped, large, high-dimensional, missing, and high-cardinality data, with equal tuning time and total compute plus latency, calibration, and energy reporting.

Claim state: Editorial hypothesis · High confidence

Partial results

  • The earlier 45-dataset benchmark found tree-based models strongest on roughly 10,000-sample datasets.
  • TabArena finds deep methods catch up with larger time budgets and ensembles while foundation models excel on smaller datasets.
  • BeyondArena finds tabular foundation models strongest on tiny-to-medium IID data while trees and conventional deep models dominate several non-IID, large, and high-dimensional regimes.

Counterexamples and limits

  • TabR beats gradient-boosted decision trees on a benchmark designed to be favorable to them.
  • TabPFN and later versions lead on several small-to-medium IID suites.
  • TabArena shows deep ensembles catch up under larger time budgets.
  • BeyondArena shows that no one family dominates across IID and non-IID regimes.

Novelty review

Status: Targeted search. Checked: 2026-08-04. arXiv, living benchmarks, and official repositories for tabular deep learning, foundation models, gradient-boosted trees, temporal and group shift, and equal-budget comparisons. The old blanket claim that trees beat deep learning is no longer supportable; confidence is high only for the revised regime-selection and non-IID robustness question.

Suggested starting points

  • Individual: Compare CatBoost, XGBoost, TabPFN, and TabM on 8-12 datasets using both random and temporal splits under an identical one-hour tuning budget.
  • Small lab: Build a preregistered 100-plus-dataset regime map including latency, energy, calibration, missingness, high-cardinality features, and temporal or group shift.
  • Well-funded lab: Train a tabular foundation model on disjoint source corpora and evaluate frozen cross-domain and non-IID transfer against the best trees and AutoGluon under total-cost parity.
